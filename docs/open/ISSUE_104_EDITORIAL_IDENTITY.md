# Issue #104 — Editorial identity for library expansion

**Status:** owner framing / research guidance  
**Issue:** #104  
**Implementation:** none

## Core idea

Argus should feel less like a catalogue of courses and more like a **curated practical miscellany of things worth knowing and being able to do**.

The childhood reference point is the spirit of *The Dangerous Book for Boys*: compact, memorable knowledge; field skills; practical competence; curiosity; things that are enjoyable to know because they may actually be useful. Argus should borrow that **editorial instinct**, not the book's audience, era, tone, gender framing, or factual standards.

The adult Argus version should be:

- evidence-based;
- source-backed;
- practical rather than academic for its own sake;
- broad across domains rather than dominated by codes, alphabets or vocabulary;
- finite enough to learn and retain;
- suitable for short mobile practice;
- scenario-based when judgement matters;
- localized to Ontario/Canada when law, regulation, emergency practice or safety guidance varies by jurisdiction;
- explicit about what app completion does and does not prove.

## Editorial test

A candidate belongs when the answer to most of these is yes:

- Would this make someone more capable in an ordinary real-world situation?
- Is it satisfying to know even when it is not immediately needed?
- Can the useful boundary be learned, practiced and retained?
- Does it add a genuinely different kind of competence to the library?
- Can Argus teach it without pretending to replace hands-on training, professional judgement or certification?

Avoid building a library that is technically diverse but cognitively repetitive. Ten different symbol systems are still one learning shape.

## Desired range

The collection should deliberately span different kinds of competence, for example:

- observation and street awareness;
- psychology and judgement;
- de-escalation and crisis communication;
- interviewing and memory;
- navigation and environmental awareness;
- emergency and safety frameworks;
- Ontario/Canadian legal and civic literacy where practically useful;
- firearm safety/legal memory support tied to formal Canadian training;
- communications and codes;
- technical fluency;
- useful natural-world identification;
- compact systems, procedures and reference knowledge.

No single family should dominate simply because it is easy to turn into flashcards.

## Product implication

Argus needs two primary learning shapes:

1. **Finite recall** — mappings, procedures, frameworks and reference knowledge.
2. **Scenario application** — observation, judgement, communication and decision practice.

A future course earns additional interaction types only when its learning objective requires them. Do not force every practical skill into a vocabulary deck and do not build bespoke course machinery for each subject.

## Tone

Competent, curious and practical. The content may be serious, but the collection should retain the pleasure of learning useful things for their own sake.

It should not drift into prepper, tactical-game, macho, alarmist, pseudo-psychological or credential-like framing.

## Decision authority

This framing should guide #104 candidate selection and any implementation issues split from it. The longer research papers provide evidence and candidate detail; this document states the editorial identity they should serve.
